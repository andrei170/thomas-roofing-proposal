You told me you measure by the year rather than the month, because some months are dead and some are flat out. That's the right way to look at it and it's how I've priced this. Fifteen extra roofs across twelve months covers the whole year's fee, which is about one a month on average, landing mostly in the months you're currently quiet.

The actual opportunity is the quiet months

You don't need more work in August. You're already flat out.

The problem with a seasonal trade isn't the annual total, it's the shape of it. The busy months are capped by how many roofs your team can physically get through, so extra enquiries then are wasted or turned away. The quiet months have spare capacity, fixed costs still going out, and wages still to pay.

Which means the entire value of this is concentrated in your troughs, not spread evenly. Filling four quiet weeks is worth far more to you than another five leads in your busiest fortnight.

One thing to correct

Your margins are not on Companies House, and that's not your fault.

You mentioned they'd be filed there. I checked company 11071653. Every set of accounts since incorporation in 2017 has been filed as total exemption full accounts, which for a company your size means a balance sheet only, with no profit and loss account. Turnover, costs and margin are all legally exempt from public filing.

So the £600k and the margin behind it are numbers only you and your accountant hold. I can't verify them and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But your accountant can tell you your gross margin per roof in about five minutes, and that one number is what decides how hard we can afford to bid for a customer. It's the most useful thing you could bring to the next conversation.
